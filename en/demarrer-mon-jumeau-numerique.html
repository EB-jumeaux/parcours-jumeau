Before you touch anything

What you're installing, and why

A ten-minute read. By the end, you'll know what each piece is for, where your twin lives, and what the two settings in step 1 change.

Have you already read "How the digital twin works"?

If so, skim this section and pick up again at the section "Your brain is yours, and no one else's". It's here for anyone who came straight to this page.

Request access to the template now

The template is the starting brain, in a private repository. Getting access to it is the only wait in the whole process. Accept the invitation GitHub sent you by email, as explained in the "Create my GitHub account" training. You have nothing to send.

A twin is a brain plus execution

The brain is a folder of text files that belongs to you. It holds who you are, how you write, what matters to you, and your current projects. It grows as you work with your twin.

Execution is Claude reading that brain and acting. The two are kept apart on purpose. You can change computers or AI models. As long as the brain follows you, your twin follows you.

This brain is backed up online, in a private repository on your GitHub account. It's your repository. The installation consists of creating it, then putting a working copy on your computer.

The pieces, and when each one is used

You only install one application, Claude. Claude installs the rest for you in step 3.

The Claude app the computer

The desktop app, signed in to your own Claude account. Your twin lives in its Code tab, on an environment you create once, called Jumeau.

The only program you open, every day.

Claude Code the twin

This is your twin itself, the engine that reads the brain and acts on your computer. There's nothing to install. Claude Code is in the app and your Claude subscription includes it.

Runs in the Code tab, at every session.

Git and GitHub the backup

Git is the mechanism that sends your brain online, and GitHub is where it's hosted. A lost, stolen or replaced computer doesn't take your twin with it. You don't touch any of this day to day.

Claude installs Git in step 3, and you never touch it again.

The GitHub tool sign-in

GitHub's official tool (gh). It signs you in to GitHub through your browser and creates your private repository. After the installation, your twin uses it without you.

Claude installs it and uses it in step 3.

Python the engine for the tools

What runs your twin's tools (reading an email with its attachments, saving a file, preparing a spreadsheet). You never use it directly. Without it, your twin has no hands.

Claude installs it in step 3, and you never touch it again.

Three tabs in the same app, and only one is the right one

The Claude app has three tabs: chat, Cowork and Code. Your twin lives in Code, and nowhere else. In the other tabs, Claude can't see your jumeau folder and can't install anything on your computer. If you paste a text from this training in the wrong place, nothing serious happens. Just start again in the Code tab.

The everyday routine, and its two settings

Open the Claude app, go to the Code tab, choose Jumeau, and write. There are no commands to remember.

Two settings go with this routine. You turn them on once, in step 1. The first one needs explaining, because its name sounds alarming.

By default, Claude asks for your permission before writing a file and before running a command. For an ordinary task, that's dozens of questions, and you end up saying yes without reading. That protects no one.

The "Allow bypass permissions mode" setting lets you choose "Bypass permissions" below the input box. Your twin then works in one go, and its work scrolls by on screen. The app remembers this choice for your jumeau folder, so you only do it once.

The "Connect new sessions to Remote Control" setting makes every session reachable beyond its window, from the web and from your phone. That way you can follow and steer your twin when you're away from your computer. Without it, the session only responds to its own window.

What this changes for you

You no longer approve each action. You watch the work as it happens. If something surprises you, you interrupt your twin and ask it what it's doing.

What doesn't change: nothing goes out without your approval. An email, a message, an entry in a tool: your twin prepares a draft and waits for your go-ahead. This rule is in its brain, and the setting doesn't touch it.

Security

Your brain is yours, and no one else's

Your twin will end up holding your projects, the way you make decisions, your emails. Here is where that content lives, who can read it, and what leaves your computer.

Two repositories, and they don't belong to the same person

There is the template, a private repository EB-jumeaux keeps as an up-to-date copy of the model designed by Pascal Jarry. It contains the skeleton, the starting instructions, the tools and the safeguards. Pascal Jarry improves it regularly, and our copy follows. It contains no one's personal content, not Éric's, not anyone else's, not yours. It's a shell.

And there is your repository, which Claude creates in step 3 on your GitHub account, as private. That's where your brain lives, and it belongs to you. No one but you has access to it, not Éric, not the template's other users. Only GitHub, which hosts it, holds it, as with any private repository.

What Éric sees of your repository

Nothing. This isn't a promise, it's a consequence of how things are built. Your repository is private and on your account, so access can only be granted from your account, by you. As long as you invite no one, no one else can read it, and you can check this at any time in your repository's settings on GitHub.

The reverse isn't true, and that's intentional. You have read access to the template, since you start from it and your twin gets its updates there. But what you read there belongs to no one in particular. It's just skeleton.

Updates only flow one way

At every startup, your twin checks the template and copies a read-only version of it to a local cache. It compares, picks up the tools improved since last time, and updates its mechanics.

It never writes anything there. Your access to the template is read-only, and GitHub would refuse any write. What you write goes to your repository, and stops there.

The update only touches the mechanics: the tools and the basic reflexes. Your projects, your profile and your memory are never replaced by the template.

What would happen if the template disappeared

Nothing serious. If the template's repository closed tomorrow, or your access to it were withdrawn, your twin would keep working exactly the same. It just wouldn't receive any more improvements.

Your brain is in your repository, your working copy is on your disk, and your Claude subscription is yours. Nothing depends on Éric.

Secrets never go in the repository

Your twin will need keys to work, for example an app password to read your mailbox. These values live in a .env file at the root, which is excluded from the repository and stays that way.

This is a rule, not a setting to check. A secret put in git is a published secret, even on a private repository. It stays in the history, and the history travels with every copy. Your twin knows this rule. It will tell you which line to fill in and where to find the value, but you write it yourself, by hand. It will never ask you to paste a key into the conversation.

What leaves your computer

  • Your conversation, and the content of the files your twin reads to answer you, go to Anthropic to be processed, as with any use of Claude.
  • Your files are also pushed to your private repository on GitHub, which hosts it. They go nowhere else.
  • What your twin sends for you, an email or a message, never goes out until you've said yes. It prepares, it waits. This rule lives in its brain, and it doesn't depend on any technical setting.

Step zero

Two accounts to create, one access to request

Two accounts are yours, and only one access comes from Éric Bourbeau. Check all three before you start.

GitHub account first, request second

Create your GitHub account before requesting access. An invitation can't be sent to someone who doesn't have an account yet. The username is needed to issue it.

What you don't need

No password to receive from anyone. You sign in to GitHub through your browser, with your own account.

Permissions on your computer

Python installs in your account, without administrator rights. Git, if it's missing, may bring up a system window asking for your confirmation. On a personal computer, you can accept. On a computer provided by an employer, check before you start, and if an installation is refused, don't work around it.

1
In the Claude app, Settings

Configure the Claude app

Five minutes, no prompt. Two settings to turn on, just once.

  1. Open the Claude app on your computerMac. If you don't have it, go to claude.ai/download, install it and sign in with your Claude account.
  2. Open Settings, then Claude Code.
  3. Turn on "Allow bypass permissions mode". Without it, Claude would ask your permission for every action.
  4. Also turn on "Connect new sessions to Remote Control". It lets you steer your session from your phone or the web.
On a personal subscription

These two settings were noted on a business account. Whether they appear on a personal Pro subscription hasn't been checked yet. If you can't find them, report it before going any further.

You're done when

The app is open on your Claude account and both settings are on.

2
On your computer

Create your twin's folder

Five minutes. No prompt, just one folder to create where you'll be able to find it again.

Your twin's brain lives in a folder on your computer. It's the only location you'll need to know.

Why you create it yourself

You create it yourself so you know where it is. This is the folder you point to in step 3.

Put it in your Documents and name it jumeau. All lowercase, with no spaces and no accents. On a PC, if your Documents are in OneDrive, put it directly in your personal folder instead (C:\Users\your name).

  1. Open File Explorer, the yellow folder icon in your taskbarFinder, the blue icon in your Dock.
  2. In the left column, click Documents.
  3. Right-click in the empty area on the right, choose New, then Folder.Open the File menu at the very top of the screen, then choose New Folder.
  4. Name it jumeau, then press Enter.

You've just created Documents\jumeauDocuments/jumeau. It's empty, which is normal. Claude fills it in step 3.

You're done when

You can see the jumeau folder in your Documents.

3
In the Claude app, Code tab

Retrieve your brain

You create the Jumeau environment, then paste a single prompt. Claude installs what's missing, has you sign in to GitHub, copies the template, creates your private repository and connects to it. All you do is sign in.

Check this before you start

Access to the template must be open. It's the third item in step zero. If you haven't yet accepted the invitation GitHub sent you by email, the clone fails with a refusal. Go accept the invitation first. It takes ten seconds.

Open your twin on your folder

  1. In the Claude app, go to the Code tab.
  2. In "Select environment", create a new environment. In the "New environment" window, stay on the Local tab. Enter Jumeau as the name, choose This computer under "Run in", then, under Folders, click "+ Add" and choose your jumeau folder. Click "Create environment".
  3. Open a new session on Jumeau. Below the input box, choose "Bypass permissions". The app will remember it for this folder.
Local, never Cloud

A Cloud environment runs on an Anthropic machine, not on your computer. It can't see your jumeau folder and installs nothing on your side. For your twin, it's always Local.

4
In the Claude app, Code tab

Your first session

The installation is done. What comes next is a conversation, and that's what makes the difference between a generic assistant and one that writes like you.

  1. In the Code tab, open a new session on Jumeau. A new one, not the installation one. A session reads the brain when it opens, and the installation one opened on an empty folder.
  2. Check that "Bypass permissions" is selected below the input box, and write Hello.
  3. Your twin introduces itself and asks you a series of questions to build your profile. Who you are, how you write, what matters to you.
  4. Answer in your working language, in ordinary sentences.
Take your time here

A twin is only as good as its profile. The most valuable part is the one about your voice. Paste three emails you wrote yourself and ask it to work out your way of writing from them.

You decide its scope

This starting brain is neutral. Tell it what you want it to cover: work, personal life, or both.

You're done when

The interview is over. Your twin has written your answers to its files and saved them in your repository. Ask it to confirm this.

Self-check

If you get stuck

Each of these cases really happened, or is flagged as unverified. Find your symptom and apply the fix. Two attempts at most, then report it and wait, without improvising repairs.

"Bypass permissions" doesn't appear below the input box

The choice only exists if this setting is on. It's the first setting in step 1.

Open Settings, Claude Code, and turn on "Allow bypass permissions mode". Then open a new session on Jumeau. If the setting doesn't exist on your subscription, report it.

Claude tells me it can't install anything, or can't see my folder

You're writing in an ordinary conversation or in Cowork. There, Claude isn't working on your computer. Only the Code tab, in Local, can see your jumeau folder.

Go to the Code tab, choose the Jumeau environment, and paste your prompt again in a new session.

My session is running in Cloud

A Cloud session runs on an Anthropic machine. It can't see your computer, your folder or your tools.

Open a new session and choose the Jumeau environment, the one you created in Local. If you can't see it, redo step 3 from the start, on the Local tab.

GitHub sign-in is stuck, or Claude says it can't finish it

This sign-in goes through your browser, and Claude can't answer a question asked in a window for you. This is the one point in the process that hasn't been tested end to end yet, and your report helps fix it.

See whether a GitHub page is waiting in your browser, or a window behind the others. If not, copy Claude's message in full and write to Éric Bourbeau, without trying another route.

The template clone is refused, or I'm told the repository doesn't exist
Repository not found.

A private repository you don't have access to looks like a repository that doesn't exist. GitHub never says "you don't have permission". So it's almost always the access that's missing, not the address that's wrong.

Check two things, in this order. Did you accept the GitHub invitation you received by email, and did the GitHub sign-in really finish? If both are done and it still fails, report it.

My repository was created but looks empty or has no main branch

The most misleading case, because everything seems to have worked. Check the branch before looking anywhere else.

Ask Claude to check the branch name, rename it to main if needed, then push again. Don't recreate the repository.

The session doesn't recognize me and answers like an ordinary Claude

A session reads the brain when it opens. The installation session opened when your folder was still empty.

Open a new session on Jumeau. If you've just installed, quit the app first and reopen it.

A message says the selected model requires usage credits

The model offered by default isn't enabled on your subscription. Nothing is broken, and it has nothing to do with the installation. The exact wording of this message in the app hasn't been recorded yet.

In the model selector, below the input box, choose a model available on your subscription, then paste your prompt again.

I dictate with Wispr Flow and the text doesn't go into the input box

Dictation in the Code tab's input box hasn't been verified yet. Your report will help verify it.

Tell Éric by email, with your system and the version of the Claude app.

An error appears and I don't understand it

You have no commands to type in this training. If you're asked to type one, something has sent you the wrong way.

Stop. Copy the error message in full, from the first line, and send it as is to Éric Bourbeau by email, with a subject that starts with Jumeau. A screenshot alone isn't enough. The exact text is what makes it possible to decide.

The folder already exists, or the clone stopped halfway

It can always be fixed, on one condition: don't delete anything.

Report it as is to Éric Bourbeau. A half-finished installation can be resumed without harm. A brain erased by mistake is hard to recover.

I reported a blocker and haven't had a reply

Two separate rules. A reported blocker gets a follow-up the very next day. A request for access to the template gets a follow-up after two business days. An unanswered question costs more days than any error screen.

Follow up the very next day, same email, same subject.

What keeps your twin useful over time.

One routine every day

Report any defect to Éric Bourbeau by email. Your content never goes up to the template, but a defect you report gets fixed there, and the fix benefits everyone.
