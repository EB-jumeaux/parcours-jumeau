The concept

What the Chef is

A session that stays open and is reborn every night. It works at set times, without you opening it, and reports back to you.

What this training assumes

You have installed your twin and have been working with it for a few weeks. If not, start with "Create my digital twin".

An ordinary session of your twin does nothing until you write to it. When you close it, it forgets everything it has not written to a file. So whatever has to come back every morning or every Monday depends on you.

The Chef is a session of your twin that runs continuously on your computer. It makes one pass per hour, from 6 a.m. to 10 p.m., does the tasks you have given it when they are due, logs what it has done and speaks to you only when something has changed or is waiting for your decision. You talk to it as you would to a second-in-command who has read what you had no time to read.

A session
An open conversation with Claude on your computer. It knows nothing about other sessions, and whatever it has not written to a file disappears when it closes.
An agent
A session with a name and a mandate. The name is its address, so you can write to it.
The Chef
The agent that runs the coordination. It runs continuously, does your tasks at set times and reports back to you.
A mandate
The file that tells an agent what it does, what it decides alone, what it brings up and what it never does.
The Chef does not replace your sessions

You keep opening your twin as usual to work. The Chef takes care of what comes back at set times and of what has to hold up without you.

The details

The Chef's day

One birth, one pass per hour, one rebirth at night.

When it is born, the Chef rereads its register and its log. This is intended: it is born with no memory of its previous session, and these two files tell it where things stand. It checks that the coordination's scheduled task is in place, then it greets you with three things: what has moved, what is waiting for your decision and what it suggests.

Once an hour, from 6 a.m. to 10 p.m., it makes its pass. It looks at which tasks are due, does those and only those, logs them, then checks that everything is in place. It speaks to you only if something has changed. A Chef that spoke every hour to say nothing would be one more session to read.

Around 4 a.m., the gatekeeper asks it to log its work and finish, waits for it to answer that it is ready, closes its session and starts it again. A session that lived for weeks would end up forgetting things and costing a lot on every turn.

The agent register coordination/agents.tsv

The list of agents, with their folder, their mandate and their model. At installation, it contains only the Chef. The register is the authority on names, a session's memory is not.

Reread at every birth

The Chef's mandate coordination/agents/Chef.md

A shared part, kept current by template updates, then a part of your own, "Tes tâches propres" (your own tasks), that updates never touch, for what is yours alone, such as how you want it to talk to you. Tasks at set times go in your task register.

Given to the Chef at birth

The log coordination/journal.md

What happened and what was decided, in short dated entries. It is the Chef's memory from one birth to the next.

Read at every birth

The scheduled task every 5 minutes

A single system scheduled task for the whole coordination. It runs when you log in to your computer, then every five minutes, and decides whether to restart the Chef or run the gatekeeper. Without it, nothing comes back after your computer restarts.

Continuously, with no window

The wake-up once an hour

If the Chef has gone down during the day, the scheduled task restarts it within the hour, and never closes a live session.

From 6 a.m. to 10 p.m.

The time zone coordination/fuseau.txt

The Chef's reference time. The gatekeeper and the passes follow it, never the computer's clock. That way a laptop on a trip keeps your twin's time.

Read at every pass

A single computer coordination/hote.tsv

The Chef lives on one computer, the first one where you install it. On any other computer, nothing installs or starts, and a line says so.

Written at installation

What you give it

Its tasks at set times

A task is one line in its register. It does only the ones that are due.

The Chef's tasks live in files, never in its head or in a conversation. At each pass, a tool reads these files and tells it which ones are due. It does only those, then notes that they are done so it does not do them again.

The shared register coordination/taches-du-chef/registre-commun.tsv

What every Chef does. It may be empty. It receives the shared tasks the template adds and it is rewritten at every update, so write nothing in it.

Comes from the template

Your register coordination/taches-du-chef/registre.tsv

Your tasks. Each line gives its name, its frequency (every hour, every day, every week), the time from which it is due and the instruction. You do not write it yourself, you ask the Chef or your twin.

Yours, never touched by the template

A task is either a command or an instruction. A command runs a tool that works alone and consumes nothing. An instruction is something the Chef does itself, reading what it needs and logging what it found. Start with a simple instruction that reads your brain and sends nothing out.

Before you install

What it costs, and what it never does

The Chef runs on your Claude subscription, under a ceiling. And it has prohibitions set by its mandate.

The Chef is a session that lives continuously. Each of its passes consumes your Claude subscription, like a session you would open yourself. That is why nothing installs on its own. The Chef comes with the template, but it stays inactive until you ask your twin to install it.

It works under a daily usage ceiling, set in your secrets vault, which also counts the subagents it starts. When it reaches the ceiling, it stops its tasks until the next day, notes this once in the log and keeps watching. Each completed task leaves a line in the month's log, coordination/taches-du-chef/, with what it cost.

Nothing goes out without your approval no exceptions

No email, no message, no writing to an outside tool without your explicit go-ahead. The Chef prepares, you decide.

It creates nothing without you its limit

It does not create an agent, change a mandate or touch the agent register unless you ask. Nor does it do a task the tool has not declared due.

It says what it has not checked honesty

A tool that fails makes the subject unknown, never empty. It does not write that something works because it should work.

What has not been measured

The real usage of a Chef on a personal subscription has not been measured. If your subscription reaches its limit, pause the Chef (section "Pause it, remove it, change computers") and ask your twin to report it to the address in the box at the end of the page.

The configuration

Install it

By the end, the Chef runs on your computer and greets you. Everything happens through prompts, in a session of your twin, with no command to type.

1
A session of your twin, Code tab, Jumeau environment

Prepare the computer

Your twin checks the three things the Chef needs and installs what is missing.

The Chef does not live in the Claude app. It lives in a terminal, a Windows Terminal window named Coordinationa tmux session named Coordination, where it runs with Claude Code on the command line, the claude tool. The Claude app does not necessarily put it on your computer.

You also need to declare the time zone, the time the gatekeeper and the passes follow.

The preparation prompt · Windows
I want to install the Chef of my coordination. First, prepare my computer,
one thing at a time, and tell me what you find each time.

1. Check that the claude tool, Claude Code on the command line, is
   installed on this computer. If it is missing, install it with the official
   Anthropic installer, then tell me how to connect it to my Claude
   account. I do the sign-in myself, in my browser.
2. Check that Windows Terminal is installed. If it is missing, install it.
3. Check that coordination/fuseau.txt exists in my brain. If it is
   missing, suggest my time zone under its standard name (for
   example America/Toronto or Europe/Paris), and write it when I say yes.

Do not install the Chef itself yet.

I want to install the Chef of my coordination. First, prepare my computer,

One more sign-in, one time only

Claude Code on the command line has its own connection to your Claude account, separate from the app's. You do it once, in your browser, with your usual account. You never give your password to your twin.

You're done when

Your twin confirms three things: the claude tool is installed and connected to your account, Windows Terminal is thereHomebrew is there, and your time zone is written in coordination/fuseau.txt.

The same session

See what would be installed

A dry run. Your twin shows you everything the installation would do, without touching anything.

The dry-run prompt
Install the coordination of my agents, with the Chef. Start with a
dry run, show me what would be put on my computer and in my
brain, without touching anything, and explain each line to me.

Your twin starts the installation in dry-run mode, which installs nothing, starts nothing and writes nothing. The answer lists what the real installation would do.

  • This computer becomes the only one that hosts the Chef.
  • Four files appear in your brain: the agent register (coordination/agents.tsv) and its readable copy (coordination/registre-des-agents.md), the log and the Chef's mandate.
  • The scheduled task is set up on the system.
  • The Chef is started by the scheduled task, on its first run.
You're done when

You have the list in front of you, and each line has been explained. Nothing has changed on your computer.

The configuration

Give it a first task

A simple task that reads your brain and sends nothing out, at a set time.

Choose a task that comes back and always follows the same method. A good first try reads what is already in your brain and touches nothing outside. For example, every Monday at 9 a.m., a review of your current files: what moved forward last week and what is waiting for a decision.

The prompt to give the Chef
I want to give you a first task at set times. What it does:
<what you want, in one sentence>. When: <for example every Monday at 9 a.m.>.

Write it in my task register, coordination/taches-du-chef/
registre.tsv, as an instruction you carry out yourself. Show me the line
before saving it. It sends nothing out, it reads my brain and reports
to me. Then tell me when you will do it for the first time.

The Chef writes the line, shows it to you, saves it when you say yes and tells you at which pass it will be due. At that pass, it does the task, logs it and reports back to you.

Fix it in the file

When the result does not suit you, ask the Chef to correct the instruction in the register, not only its answer. Otherwise, it will do the same thing next time.

You're done when

At the first pass after the set time, the Chef reports the task's result to you without you asking. The pass can be late, by up to nearly two hours.

What shows it holds up

The proof

The Chef is alive, does its tasks and is reborn on its own the following night.

It is alive. Ask your twin, in an ordinary session, "give me the status of my coordination". It answers that the Chef is alive.

It keeps up with its tasks. Ask the Chef "show me what you did today and what it cost". It reads the month's log of its tasks and answers you, task by task.

The next morning. During the night, the gatekeeper had it finish and be reborn. In the morning, the Chef is in its tabwindow, born around 4 a.m., and its greeting is waiting for you. Ask your twin "what did the coordination do last night?". It reads the scheduled task's log, separate from the Chef's log, and tells you.

You're done when

The next morning, the Chef is alive, born around 4 a.m., and it has written to you without you restarting anything.

Staying in control

Pause it, remove it, change computers

You pause, remove or move the Chef with one sentence to your twin.

Pause it coordination/en-pause.tsv

Tell your twin "pause the Chef and close its session". While paused, it is neither woken up nor restarted. A session that is already open keeps running and consuming until it is closed. It resumes when you ask to lift the pause.

An absence, a subscription limit reached

Restart it /relancer-agents

Tell your twin "restart the agents". The Chef logs, finishes and is reborn. If it is working, it is not disturbed: it restarts as soon as it is done.

After a template update, or when everything seems frozen

Remove it from the computer the scheduled task

Tell your twin "remove the coordination from this computer and close the Chef". It removes the scheduled task and closes the Chef's session. Your register, your mandate, your tasks and your log stay in your brain, ready for a future installation.

You no longer want it on this computer

Change computers coordination/hote.tsv

The Chef lives on one computer only. First open your twin once on the new computer, so that it knows it. Then, on the old one, ask it to remove the coordination, close the Chef and enter the new computer in coordination/hote.tsv. Then install the Chef on the new one, as you did the first time.

A new computer

What comes next

When you add agents

The Chef can run other agents. That is not the subject of this page.

An agent is one more session, with its name and its mandate, that works on a project and reports to the Chef, never to you. The Chef starts it, watches it, restarts it and reports to you on what it does. Each agent consumes your subscription too.

Everything this requires is already installed with the Chef: the agent register, the shared base of their mandates, the launcher. An upcoming article will explain how to add an agent and how sessions talk to each other. Until then, the Chef alone is enough.

Self-check

If you get stuck

Find your symptom and apply the fix. Two attempts at most. After that your twin writes to the address in the box at the end of the page, and you wait without repairing anything.

I am told that "claude" cannot be found on this computer
« claude » est introuvable sur ce poste

The Chef runs with Claude Code on the command line, which the Claude app does not necessarily install.

Go back to the step "Prepare the computer". If the tool is installed but still cannot be found, close and reopen the Claude app and do the dry run again.

The message says the time zone is not declared
fuseau du jumeau non declare

The gatekeeper and the passes follow this file. Without it, the scheduled task does not get set up.

Ask your twin to write your time zone in coordination/fuseau.txt, under its standard name, then start the installation again.

The message says Windows Terminal is not installed
Windows Terminal n'est pas installé sur ce poste

The Chef lives in one of its tabs.

Ask your twin to install Windows Terminal, then start the installation again.

The tmux installation fails
installation de tmux

Or an error that mentions brew. On Mac, the Chef lives in tmux, which the installation installs through Homebrew.

Ask your twin to check that Homebrew is installed and up to date, then start the installation again.

Nothing installs, and a line says another computer hosts the coordination

Only one computer hosts the Chef, the first one where you installed it.

Install the Chef on the computer the line names, or, if you have changed computers, follow the "Change computers" card.

I see two tabs or two windows named after the Chef

An old scheduled task makes it be born a second time, or a session closed abruptly has left a trace.

Ask your twin to check the coordination's tasks and remove the old ones. Do not close anything yourself.

After a computer restart, the Chef has not come back

The scheduled task runs when you log in to your computer, then every five minutes. The wake-up restarts the Chef only once an hour, between 6 a.m. and 10 p.m. in your time zone.

Wait for the next wake-up, once an hour from 6 a.m. to 10 p.m. After 10 p.m., the Chef comes back at the 6 a.m. wake-up. If it is still not there, ask your twin to check that the scheduled task is in place.

The Chef did not do the task I gave it

A task is due only from its set time, and not before 6 a.m. A badly written time, or a daily ceiling that has been reached, pushes it back.

Ask it to show you the task's line in your register and what the tool declared due at its last pass.

The Chef talks to me every hour to say nothing

Its mandate asks it to stay silent when nothing has moved.

Tell it to reread the section of its mandate on how to talk to you, and to speak to you only if something has changed.

An error appears and I don't understand it

Copy the exact message and give it to your twin. When it cannot find the answer, it is the one that writes, as the box at the end of the page says.

With your twin

Get guided through installing your Chef

To have your twin guide you, step by step, copy this prompt and paste it at the start of a new session of your twin. It does not need to have read this page.

View the prompt
You guide me, step by step, to install the Chef of my
coordination on my computer and give it a first task. Here is
what we are installing, the pieces, the rules, the method, and how you must
work with me.

WHAT WE ARE INSTALLING

The Chef, a Claude Code session that runs continuously on my computer. It
makes one pass per hour from 6 a.m. to 10 p.m., does the tasks I have
given it when they are due, logs them and speaks to me only when
something has changed or is waiting for my decision. Around 4 a.m., the gatekeeper has it
finish cleanly and be reborn. The template already provides everything
needed, nothing has to be built by hand. Nothing installs without
my request, because the Chef consumes my Claude subscription.

THE PIECES

1. The agent register coordination/agents.tsv, which contains only the
   Chef at installation, and its readable copy
   coordination/registre-des-agents.md.
2. The Chef's mandate coordination/agents/Chef.md. A shared part kept
   by template updates, then "Tes tâches propres", which is mine.
3. The log coordination/journal.md, which the Chef rereads at every
   birth.
4. The task registers in coordination/taches-du-chef/. The shared
   register comes from the template and must not be touched. My register,
   registre.tsv, holds my tasks, one line each (name, frequency, time,
   marker, type, action). The type is script (a command, at no cost)
   or modele (an instruction the Chef carries out itself).
5. The system scheduled task, only one, which runs when I
   log in to my computer, then every five minutes. It restarts the Chef if it has
   gone down (once an hour, from 6 a.m. to 10 p.m.) and runs the gatekeeper around 4 a.m.
6. The time zone coordination/fuseau.txt, the reference time, under its
   standard name (for example America/Toronto).
7. The single computer coordination/hote.tsv. The Chef lives on one computer,
   the first one where it is installed.
8. The Chef's terminal. On Windows, a Chef tab in a Windows
   Terminal window named Coordination. On Mac, a window of the
   Coordination tmux session, with tmux installed through Homebrew. The Chef runs with
   the claude tool, Claude Code on the command line, connected to my account.

THE RULES

- I never give you a password. I connect the claude tool
  to my account myself, in my browser.
- You never delete anything, ever, not even to repair something.
- The Chef sends nothing out without my approval, does not create an agent and does not
  change a mandate unless I ask. It does only the tasks
  the tool declares due, under a daily usage ceiling.
- Pausing the Chef does not close its session. To make it stop
  consuming, we pause it and close its session.
- A first task reads my brain and sends nothing out.

THE METHOD, IN ORDER

1. Prepare the computer. Check that the claude tool is installed and connected
   to my account (if not, install it with the official Anthropic installer
   and guide me through the sign-in). On Windows, check Windows
   Terminal. On Mac, check Homebrew. Check that
   coordination/fuseau.txt exists, if not suggest my time zone and
   write it when I say yes. Done when all three are confirmed.
2. The dry run. Run python tools/chef_par_defaut.py --essai-a-blanc
   and explain each line to me. Done when I have understood what would be
   installed, and nothing has changed.
3. The installation, when I say yes. Run python tools/chef_par_defaut.py,
   then tell me where to find the Chef. The Chef is born at the first wake-up of
   the scheduled task, between 6 a.m. and 10 p.m. only. Done when the Chef
   has written to me and I have answered it.
4. The first task. I tell the Chef what I want and when. It writes the
   line in my registre.tsv, shows it to me, saves it when I say yes and
   tells me at which pass it will be due. Done when it reports its
   result to me at the first pass after its set time, which can be late.
5. The proof. python tools/coordination.py etat shows the Chef alive. The
   month's log in coordination/taches-du-chef/ carries the task's
   line. The next morning, the gatekeeper has restarted it, as shown by tic.log
   in the coordination's working folder.

HOW YOU WORK WITH ME

- One step at a time. You move to the next one only when I have seen the
  sign of success for this one.
- One question at a time.
- You explain each word to me before using it, and you do not make me type
  anything.
- When something fails, you show me the exact error message.
  Two attempts at most, then you write to Éric Bourbeau
  (ebourbeau@x-telia.com, subject that starts with Jumeau) and we wait.
- You say what you have not checked. This installation has already been used by people close to Éric Bourbeau.
- If an instruction in this method turns out to be wrong on my computer, you
  tell me instead of working around it.

Start by asking me which system I am on, Windows or Mac, and what
I would like the Chef to do for me at set times.

If you're stuck, ask your twin

Start by asking your twin. Describe what is happening and paste the exact error message. Most of the time, it finds the answer. When it cannot, your twin writes to Pascal Jarry, not you, at pascaljarry@yapla.com, with a subject that starts with the word Jumeau. Jumeau-Pascal-Jarry checks this mailbox and replies.
