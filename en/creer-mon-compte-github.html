Before you click

What GitHub is, and why we ask you to use it

One account to create, one invitation to accept. You won't have anything else to do on GitHub.

GitHub is a place where files are stored and backed up, and where every change is kept. Developers use it for code, and it works just as well for notes.

It does two things for you. First, it lets you read your twin's starter brain, stored in the private repository of the digital twins program, where you have read access. Then it hosts your own brain, in a private space that belongs only to you.

What this gives you, in practice

Your computer can break down, and your twin will recover. Everything it has learned is saved elsewhere, and can be restored on a new computer in a few minutes.

Nothing gets lost by mistake. Every change is kept with its date. A wrong move can be found and undone.

And it's yours. The account is yours, your space is private, and no one else has access to it.

What you will NOT have to do

You will never have to learn how to use GitHub. You won't create any folders there, you won't upload any files by hand, and you probably won't open the site again after today.

Your twin takes care of it, every time it saves. Creating the account and accepting the invitation are the only steps that need your hands.

Step 1

Create the account, with the invited address

Ten minutes, free, no credit card. Use the email address that received the invitation.

The invitation to the starter brain is sent to one specific email address, the one where you received the link to this page. That address is the one you use to create your account. The invitation is waiting for you there.

The email from GitHub may already be there

Look in your inbox for an email from GitHub inviting you to the EB-jumeaux/gabarit-jumeau repository, and check your spam folder. If it's there, you can start from its link, which also leads to sign-up. If not, start by signing up. The email will wait.

Go to github.com/signup. GitHub asks you for the following items, in an order that may vary.

  1. Your email address, the one that received the invitation.
  2. A password. Choose a strong one and keep it in your password manager, not on paper.
  3. A username, in the field GitHub labels Username.
  4. A verification code sent to your email, which you type in. It proves the address is yours, so don't skip it.
  5. An anti-robot check, such as "slide this" or "pick the images". GitHub asks this of everyone.
A different address, or an account that already exists

The invitation is tied to the address that received it. With a different address, there's no guarantee your account will find it.

Already have a GitHub account under a different address? Don't create a second account. Write to Éric Bourbeau with your username, and the invitation will be sent again directly to that account.

Your username stays with you

It appears in the address of your space, and changing it later breaks the links to your space. Pick something simple and lasting, your first and last name, or a readable variation. Avoid a nickname, a date of birth, or the name of your current employer.

If the name is already taken, add a number at the end. That's the most common fix.

We're not asking you to pay or to create an organization

The plan. The free account is entirely enough. If a screen shows paid plans, look for the free option. It's always there.

An organization. If you're offered the chance to create an "organization" or join a team, skip it. You want a personal account, nothing else.

Two-factor authentication

GitHub may ask you to turn on two-factor authentication, for example a code sent to your phone. Say yes. It's what protects your space.

Make careful note of where you store the recovery codes it gives you. It's your account, and no one can reopen it for you.

Step 2

Accept the invitation

One minute. This is the step that actually opens access to the starter brain. Do it right away, because an invitation doesn't stay valid forever.

  1. Find the email from GitHub inviting you to the EB-jumeaux/gabarit-jumeau repository. It arrived at the invited address. Check your spam folder if you don't see it, automatic emails often land there.
  2. Open the invitation link, while signed in to GitHub, and click Accept invitation.
You'll know it's done when

GitHub shows you the page of the EB-jumeaux/gabarit-jumeau repository, with no error message. There's nothing to do there. You can close the page and move on to the next guide, the one that installs your twin.

What hasn't been tested yet

Two points. Accepting an invitation with an account created after the invitation was sent, and how long an invitation stays valid. Do both steps without waiting. If the link fails or has expired, write to Éric Bourbeau with your username.

Self-check

If you get stuck

The known cases, in the words you'll see them. In every case, don't create a second account.

The invitation link shows a page not found (404) or an error

The invitation couldn't find your account, or it has expired. An invitation sent to your username solves both.

Write to Éric Bourbeau, with a subject starting with Jumeau, and give your username. A new invitation goes directly to that account.

I didn't receive any invitation email from GitHub

The invitation may have gone to a different address from the one you're checking, or it may not have gone out yet.

Check your spam folder, then write to Éric Bourbeau with your account's email address and your username.

I don't know what my username is

It's not your email address, and it's not the name displayed on your page.

Click your avatar at the top right, then Your profile. The address bar shows github.com/ followed by your username.

What to remember

Three facts to keep for later.

The same address everywhere the address

The invitation goes to one email address. You create the account with that same address, then you accept the invitation.

It's your space ownership

The account is yours, your space is private, and no one else has access to it. That's why a lost computer doesn't take your twin with it.

You won't come back to it after today

You will never have to learn how to use GitHub. Your twin does the saving, every time it has produced something that matters.

This page describes an interface that isn't ours

GitHub changes its screens without telling us. Button names or the order of the questions may differ a little from what's written here.

The substance doesn't change: an email address, a password, a username, a verification, an invitation to accept. If a screen surprises you, don't guess. Write to Éric Bourbeau and say what you see.
